# Phase 2: Lebanese Judicial Decisions — Exhaustive Source Catalog
**اجتهادات قضائية / Jurisprudence libanaise**

*Compiled: March 2026 | Priority: HIGHEST — Sources for litigation-grade defense research*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Lebanese judicial decisions are notoriously difficult to access online. There is **no centralized, free, comprehensive government database** of court decisions equivalent to CanLII (Canada) or Légifrance (France). Access falls into three tiers:

1. **Free but sparse:** Constitutional Council (cc.gov.lb), State Council (statecouncil.gov.lb), AHJUCAF selected Cassation grands arrêts, Cyrilla.org (selected decisions), Legal Agenda commentary, Mahkama.net (news-style), STL archive at Stanford
2. **Subscription commercial databases:** Sader Legal, IDREL/Cassandre, Al-Mostachar, Al-Nashra, Hatem — covering thousands of decisions across all ordinary courts
3. **Inaccessible/offline only:** Military Tribunal, Judicial Council, Religious courts, Bar disciplinary decisions — virtually no online access

## SOURCE 1: Court of Cassation (محكمة التمييز)

**Arabic:** محكمة التمييز اللبنانية  
**French:** Cour de Cassation du Liban  
**English:** Lebanese Court of Cassation / Supreme Court

### 1A. AHJUCAF — Grands Arrêts de la Cour de Cassation
- **URL:** https://www.ahjucaf.org/liban-les-grands-arrets
- **Description:** Selected landmark decisions of the Lebanese Court of Cassation, curated by the association of supreme courts of French-speaking countries. Includes recent impactful decisions and historical "grands arrêts" presented at the March 2023 Rabat seminar on the history of supreme courts.
- **Approximate Decisions Available:** Small curated selection (estimated <50 decisions); not a comprehensive database
- **Date Range:** Primarily recent decisions + historical landmarks; no specific date range published
- **Languages:** Arabic and French
- **Anonymized or Named:** Named parties (per Lebanese practice)
- **Search Functionality:** Browse only (no keyword search)
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text of selected decisions including ratio decidendi
- **Access:** Free, open access
- **Format:** HTML/PDF
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★☆ — Curated by official judicial association; small but high-quality selection

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- Contains landmark rulings that may include acquittals and overturned convictions
- Historical grands arrêts show evolution of criminal procedure doctrine
- French-language presentation facilitates comparative analysis with French jurisprudence
- Useful for identifying procedural rules and due process standards

---

### 1B. SADER Legal — Jurisprudence Database (Subscription)
- **URL:** https://www.saderlegal.com
- **Description:** Lebanon's premier commercial legal publisher (est. 1863). Offers "Al-Qararaat Al-Madaniya — Mahkamat Al-Tamyiz" (Civil Cassation Decisions) and criminal Cassation decisions. The IJS library lists Sader as providing: (1) Civil Cassation decisions, (2) Lebanese laws, (3) comparative jurisprudence. Sader publishes periodic compilations including *قرارات محكمة التمييز الجزائية* (Criminal Court of Cassation decisions).
- **Approximate Decisions Available:** Thousands; comprehensive multi-decade coverage
- **Date Range:** Extensive historical coverage; criminal Cassation 2021 volume confirmed; estimated coverage from ~1950s to present
- **Languages:** Arabic (primary); some French
- **Anonymized or Named:** Named parties
- **Search Functionality:** Full-text keyword, by article number, by date, by case number (database version)
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text with complete legal reasoning
- **Access:** **Subscription required** — Annual fee; acquired by law offices, judges, and public entities; also available in CD-ROM/print format
- **Format:** Database (online), CD-ROM, PDF, print volumes
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★★ — Industry standard; most widely used by Lebanese lawyers and judges

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- Criminal Cassation decisions document acquittals and overturned convictions with full reasoning
- Reveals procedural errors the Cassation has found reversible — critical for identifying grounds to challenge lower court decisions
- Contains decisions on detention limits, interrogation rights, evidence admissibility
- Shows Cassation's interpretation of Criminal Procedure Code articles
- Access to criminal chamber decisions by year useful for identifying favorable panels

### 1C. IDREL — Cassandre / Manal Software (Subscription/CD-ROM)
- **URL:** http://www.idrel.com.lb (Institute for Documentation and Research on Lebanon)
- **Description:** Lebanese court decisions database with software "Al-Marjaa Cassandre" allowing full-text searches on more than 12,000 court decisions. Also includes "Manal" — an index of Lebanese jurisprudence texts. Listed in the IJS (Institute of Judicial Studies) official library resources as a key jurisprudence tool.
- **Approximate Decisions Available:** 12,000+ decisions (confirmed figure from ArabO directory listing)
- **Date Range:** Multi-decade; exact range unconfirmed but appears to cover 1970s–2020s
- **Languages:** Arabic
- **Anonymized or Named:** Named parties
- **Search Functionality:** Full-text search by keyword; index by court and article
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text including legal reasoning
- **Access:** **Subscription/Purchase required** — CD-ROM and/or database license; available to law offices and judicial institutions
- **Format:** Database software (Cassandre), CD-ROM, index tool (Manal)
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★☆ — Widely cited in academic legal research (confirmed citation in AUB legal research papers)

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- 12,000+ decisions covers a wide cross-section of all Lebanese courts (ordinary + exceptional)
- Full-text search by article number enables finding all decisions interpreting specific Criminal Procedure Code articles — essential for procedural defenses
- Historical depth allows tracking evolution of doctrine and identifying outlier decisions favorable to defense

## SOURCE 2: Constitutional Council (المجلس الدستوري)

**Arabic:** المجلس الدستوري اللبناني  
**French:** Conseil Constitutionnel du Liban  
**English:** Lebanese Constitutional Council

### 2A. Official Website of the Constitutional Council
- **URL:** https://www.cc.gov.lb/en/
- **Description:** The Constitutional Council's official website. Contains decisions on constitutionality review and disputes over parliamentary and presidential elections. The website is available in English, French, and Arabic. Most decisions are not available in English — primarily Arabic and French.
- **Approximate Decisions Available:** All official decisions since the Council's establishment in 1994; estimated 100–200 decisions total (the Council decides relatively few cases per year)
- **Date Range:** 1994–present (Council established pursuant to Taif Agreement, Law of 1993)
- **Languages:** Arabic (primary), French (some), limited English
- **Anonymized or Named:** Decisions reference referring parties by name (MPs, PM, etc.) but complainants in election cases may be named
- **Search Functionality:** Browse by category (Constitutionality Review / Parliamentary Election Disputes / Presidential Election Disputes); basic date-based navigation; search function available at https://cc.gov.lb/en/search/
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text with constitutional reasoning
- **Access:** Free, open access
- **Format:** HTML and PDF
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★★ — Official government source; authoritative

**Key Decisions Available (Examples):**
- Decision No. 7/2014 (Nov. 28, 2014) — available on site
- Decision No. 1/2013 (May 13, 2013) — available on site
- Decision No. 3/2024 and 4/2024 (April 2024) — Banking provisions; published in Official Gazette
- Decision invalidating articles of 2025 budget (May 2025) — Articles 5, 17, 22, 25, 54, 56 annulled
- Decision striking down Judicial Independence Law (February 2026) — constitution-based grounds

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- **CRITICAL FOR DEFENSE:** Contains decisions annulling legislation on constitutional grounds — provides precedent for constitutional challenges to criminal statutes
- Decisions on personal liberty guarantees (Article 8 of Constitution) can support challenges to arbitrary detention
- Constitutional Council decisions on due process and fair trial rights (incorporated via 1990 preamble adopting UDHR)
- Decisions on banking restructuring law potentially affect prosecution of financial crime defendants
- Decisions on judicial independence law (Feb. 2026) directly relevant to challenging judicial impartiality

## SOURCE 3: Council of State / Shura Council (مجلس شورى الدولة)

**Arabic:** مجلس شورى الدولة  
**French:** Conseil d'État libanais  
**English:** Lebanese Council of State / State Shura Council

### 3A. Official Website of the State Council
- **URL:** http://statecouncil.gov.lb
- **URL (Decisions section):** http://statecouncil.gov.lb/TreeView.aspx
- **Description:** Lebanon's sole administrative court (established by Law No. 10434 of June 14, 1975). The website has a search interface for decisions with fields for date, number, and base number. The decisions section states "لا جديد عن مجلس شورى الدولة بعد" (no new content) — suggesting the online publication is sporadic or inactive. The Council has 7 departments including 5 judicial chambers plus the Council of Cases (headed by the President).
- **Approximate Decisions Available:** Limited online; the most notable/recent decisions are published selectively. The Council reportedly has a large backlog of cases.
- **Date Range:** Selected decisions from 1970s–present available in print/databases; online portal coverage uncertain
- **Languages:** Arabic
- **Anonymized or Named:** Named parties
- **Search Functionality:** Search by date, number, and base number (on the portal, even if sparsely populated)
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text when available
- **Access:** Free online (portal); comprehensive coverage requires subscription databases
- **Format:** HTML (online portal); print volumes (comprehensive)
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★☆☆ for online access; ★★★★★ for official authority

**Key Recent Decisions (from news sources):**
- Decision No. 421/2017-2018 (Feb. 8, 2018): Annulled Public Security Directorate's decision on Syrian entry requirements — important precedent on administrative responsibility limits
- Ziad Itani torture case (Decision issued Dec. 5, 2024, made public March 10, 2025): Ordered government to pay reparations for failure to prevent torture; acknowledged state failure to implement anti-torture law — landmark decision
- Decision annulling financial sector recovery strategy (2025/2026): Annulled the portion eliminating Banque du Liban's foreign currency obligations
- Potential decision to oust 26 State Security officers (referenced in Mahkama.net)

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- **HIGH VALUE FOR DEFENSE:** Ziad Itani torture reparations decision (2024/2025) is landmark precedent establishing state liability for torture, failure to investigate — directly usable to challenge security service conduct in criminal prosecutions
- Annulment decisions against General Directorate of Public Security actions precedent for challenging detention orders and entry bans
- Administrative decisions limiting state power provide doctrinal framework for challenging prosecutorial overreach
- Historical decisions show Council's willingness to annul government decisions affecting civil liberties
- Contains precedents on fair trial in administrative proceedings applicable to ordinary court proceedings

## SOURCE 6: Military Tribunal (المحكمة العسكرية)

**Arabic:** المحكمة العسكرية الدائمة / محكمة التمييز العسكرية  
**French:** Tribunal militaire permanent / Cour de cassation militaire  
**English:** Permanent Military Court / Military Court of Cassation

### 6A. Official Status
- **Official Website:** None publicly accessible with decisions
- **Justice.gov.lb Reference:** https://www.justice.gov.lb/index.php/search/1/3/2 — lists Military Courts as a category but does not publish decisions
- **Decisions Available Online:** **Virtually none.** Military Tribunal decisions are not systematically published online. Individual decisions become known only through news reports (L'Orient Today, Assafir, Al-Akhbar, Mahkama.net) or academic/NGO analyses.

### 6B. Academic and NGO Documentation Sources
- **ICJ Memorandum (2018):** https://www.icj.org/resource/lebanon-restrict-military-court-jurisdiction-ensure-fair-trial-rights/ — analyzes military court procedures and landmark cases
- **Amnesty International Reports:** https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/campaigns/2024/07/inside-lebanons-military-court-no-place-for-civilians/ — documents specific military court cases including the Bashar Abed Saud torture case trial (2024)
- **Asharq Al-Awsat reporting (March 2026):** Documents Hezbollah weapons case — military court imposed symbolic fine of LL 900,000 (~$10) on each accused; government commissioner (Judge Claude Ghanem) immediately appealed to Military Cassation
- **Middle East Online (Feb. 2026):** Documents Zaiter drug kingpin case — Permanent Military Court handed one-month sentence; widespread criticism

### 6C. Key Characteristics (from NGO/Academic Sources)
- **Jurisdiction:** All crimes committed by military personnel + crimes against military + terrorism cases + many security-related offenses involving civilians
- **Composition:** Panel of military officers + civilian judges (appointed by Minister of National Defense on proposal of Ministry of Justice)
- **Civilian Defendants:** Military court regularly tries civilians — criticized by ICJ, HRW, Amnesty; UN HRC has called for restriction of this jurisdiction
- **Appeals:** Military Court of Cassation (separate from ordinary Court of Cassation)
- **Transparency:** Very low — proceedings often closed; decisions not published
- **Date Range of Available Decisions:** Scattered references in NGO reports from 1990s–2026
- **Languages:** Arabic
- **Format:** Paper only (not digitized)
- **Reliability Rating (as a source for defense):** N/A — decisions unavailable; **reliability of court itself is low** per ICJ/Amnesty assessments

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- **CRITICAL FOR DEFENSE IN SECURITY CASES:** Military courts routinely try civilians in breach of international fair trial standards — this creates constitutional challenge grounds
- ICJ memoranda document specific procedural violations: no right for victim's lawyer to address judge; limited defense counsel access; military judge composition compromises independence
- Documented cases of lenient sentences for politically connected defendants vs. harsh treatment of critics — reveals double standards useful in proportionality/equality arguments
- Recent 2026 case (Hezbollah weapons) shows Government Commissioner appealing excessively lenient sentences — precedent for prosecution appeals
- Fadl Shaker case (cited in news): tried for 2013 Abra clashes; still pending despite acquittal of fighting-the-army charge — shows arbitrary detention patterns
- **Key legal argument:** Article 6(1) ICCPR and ECHR apply to military court proceedings affecting fundamental rights

## SOURCE 7: Special Tribunal for Lebanon (STL) — المحكمة الدولية الخاصة بلبنان

**Arabic:** المحكمة الخاصة بلبنان  
**French:** Tribunal Spécial pour le Liban  
**English:** Special Tribunal for Lebanon

*The STL was closed on December 31, 2023. All records have been transferred to permanent archives.*

### 7A. Stanford University Virtual Tribunals — Primary Archive (BEST SOURCE)
- **URL:** https://virtualtribunals.stanford.edu (search for STL collection)
- **Description:** Stanford's Virtual Tribunals initiative preserved the entire STL digital record — 15,000+ files — after the tribunal's closure in December 2023. The collection was announced complete on August 27, 2025. Includes judgments, decisions, orders, transcripts, indictments, motions, filings, photographs, video, and the archived website. The 64 most historically significant files were released in June 2024; full collection released August 2025.
- **Approximate Documents Available:** 15,000+ files
- **Document Types:** Judgments, interlocutory decisions, pre-trial orders, indictments, prosecution filings, defense filings, victim representative filings, contempt decisions, appeal decisions, transcripts of public hearings, Rules of Procedure and Evidence, Statute, Annual Reports, photographs, video footage
- **Date Range:** 2007 (establishment) — 2023 (closure); cases cover events from October 2004–December 2005
- **Languages:** English, French, Arabic (most documents available in all three languages)
- **Anonymized or Named:** Named accused throughout
- **Search Functionality:** Faceted browsing + keyword search; Stanford-added metadata for enhanced discoverability
- **Reasoning Included:** Full text of all judgments with complete legal reasoning
- **Access:** Free, open access
- **Format:** PDF, digital files
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★★ — Complete official archive, expertly preserved

### 7B. ICC Legal Tools Database — STL Decisions
- **URL:** https://www.legal-tools.org (search "STL")
- **Description:** The ICC Legal Tools Database contains STL case documents including the main Ayyash et al. judgment (STL-11-01/T/TC/F3839, August 18, 2020 — 2,600+ pages).
- **Access:** Free
- **Format:** PDF
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★★

### 7C. WorldCourts.com — STL Decisions by Year
- **URL:** https://www.worldcourts.com/stl/eng/
- **Description:** Organized by year from 2001–2020. Contains PDF downloads of STL decisions.
- **Access:** Free
- **Format:** PDF
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★☆

### 7D. UN Archives and Records Management Section
- **URL:** Contact through UN Archives
- **Description:** Official custodian of STL records after closure. Less accessible than Stanford collection.
- **Access:** Request-based
- **Reliability Rating:** ★★★★★ (official custodian)

### 7E. Key STL Cases and Documents Available

| Case | Case No. | Significance |
|------|----------|--------------|
| Prosecutor v. Ayyash et al. | STL-11-01 | Main Hariri assassination case; 2020 judgment (2,600 pp.) finding Ayyash guilty in absentia |

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- **EXTRAORDINARY VALUE FOR DEFENSE:** STL proceedings apply Lebanese criminal law to international standards — creates a parallel jurisprudence applying Lebanese statutes with due process guarantees
- Interlocutory Decision on Applicable Law (Feb. 2011) provides authoritative analysis of Lebanese law on conspiracy, homicide modes, and terrorism that Lebanese defense counsel can cite before domestic courts
- STL Rules of Procedure and Evidence (reflecting Lebanon + international standards) provide benchmarks for challenging deficient domestic procedure
- STL's evidentiary standards (presumption of innocence, standard of proof, hearsay rules, circumstantial evidence doctrine) can be cited to challenge evidentiary practices in Lebanese criminal courts
- Contains decisions on right of accused to access evidence (El-Sayed case) — applicable to domestic disclosure challenges
- STL contempt decisions establish that legal entities can be tried for contempt — provides framework for understanding press/media court jurisdiction in Lebanon
- Defense counsel submissions available in full — model briefs arguing Lebanese law to international standards
- **Key practical use:** STL's Feb. 2011 analysis of Lebanese terrorism statutes is the most authoritative English-language treatment of Lebanese Criminal Code arts. 314–316 and the terrorism law

## SOURCE 8: Religious / Confessional Courts (المحاكم الدينية والطائفية)

**Arabic:** المحاكم الشرعية السنية، المحاكم الجعفرية، المحاكم الدرزية، المحاكم الروحية المسيحية  
**English:** Sunni Sharia Courts, Jaafarite Courts, Druze Courts, Christian Ecclesiastical Courts

### 8A. General Framework
Lebanon's 18 officially recognized religious communities each have their own personal status courts with exclusive jurisdiction over marriage, divorce, child custody, and inheritance for members of that community. There is **no online publicly accessible database** of religious court decisions from any denomination. Records remain in physical court registries.

### 8B. Sunni Sharia Courts (المحاكم الشرعية السنية)
- **Governing Body:** Dar el-Fatwa / High Islamic Council (Supreme Sunni Sharia Court)
- **Court Network (from FamilySearch/official sources):**
  - Beirut: Tel. 961-1-856344/856345, Tarik Jedide area
  - Tripoli (North)
  - Minieh, Sir, Halba-Aakkar, Al-Bire (North)
  - Jeb Jenine, Zahleh, Chtaura, Baalbek, Rachaiya (Bekaa)
  - Baabda-Ghbayreh, Chhim, Jiyeh-Barja, Jbeil (Mount Lebanon)

## SOURCE 9: Judicial Council (المجلس العدلي)

**Arabic:** المجلس العدلي  
**French:** Conseil de Justice  
**English:** Judicial Council (Political/Security Cases)

### 9A. Status and Characteristics
The Judicial Council is a special court of five judges designated for "political crimes" and cases involving state security referred to it by Cabinet Decree. It has no right of appeal — decisions are final. It has tried the most sensitive political/security cases in Lebanese history.

- **Official Website:** None; Ministry of Justice reference at https://www.justice.gov.lb/index.php/court-details/35/1 describes it: "Once passed, the decisions of the council are immediately referred to the Minister of Justice. Serial minutes of the council meetings and its decisions are kept..."
- **Decisions Available Online:** **None.** No Judicial Council decisions are publicly accessible online.
- **Composition:** Five judges designated by Cabinet Decree upon proposal of the Minister of Justice after consultation with the Supreme Judicial Council
- **Jurisdiction:** Crimes against state security; assassinations of political figures; some terrorism cases; any case the Cabinet decides to refer
- **Governing Law:** Lebanese Judiciary Organization Law (Decree-Law No. 150/83), Articles governing the Judicial Council

### 9B. Key Historical Cases (from news/academic sources)
- Samir Geagea (LF leader) — convicted 1994; sentenced to death (commuted); pardoned 2005
- Habib Shartouni and Nabil Alam (1982 Bashir Gemayel assassination) — Judicial Council case
- Tripoli events 2013/2014 — Cabinet referred case to Judicial Council (Facebook post, March 2026 indicates Cabinet referred Tripoli crime to Judicial Council in 2026)
- March 2026: Cabinet decision to refer "Tripoli crime" to the Judicial Council

**DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS:**
- **CRITICAL RISK ALERT:** Referral to the Judicial Council removes all right of appeal — this is the highest-risk procedural status for any defendant
- There is **no published jurisprudence** from the Judicial Council; it operates essentially in secret
- The absence of appellate review is itself a human rights challenge ground before international bodies (HRC, ECHR if individual brings case via another state)
- ICJ and HRW have documented that Judicial Council proceedings do not meet fair trial standards
- Cabinet's power to refer cases to the Judicial Council is a prosecutorial/executive tool used for political cases — challenging the Cabinet referral decree before the State Council has been attempted (outcome: jurisdiction disputed)
- **The fact that no decisions are published is itself a defense argument** — lack of judicial transparency violates right to public trial

### 10A. Banking Special Tribunal

## CONSOLIDATED DEFENSE STRATEGY ANNOTATIONS

### Precedents Limiting Prosecutorial Power
1. **Shura Council — Ziad Itani decision (Dec. 2024/Mar. 2025):** State liability for torture by investigators; Shura Council held Lebanon failed to implement anti-torture law — directly limits security interrogation practices
2. **STL Interlocutory Decision (Feb. 2011):** Established that conspiracy requires agreement + overt act; limits broad conspiracy charges under Lebanese law
3. **Court of Cassation statutory limitations decisions:** Decision No. 210/1983 (limitation is public order, can be raised at any stage); Decisions No. 53 and 55/2002; Criminal Cassation 2/8/2010 and 21/1/2010 — strictly enforcing prescription periods cuts off prosecution
4. **Cassation Decision No. 230:** Public prosecution extinguished after 10 years from last procedural act in felony — critical for stale prosecutions

### Acquittals / Overturned Convictions
- Al-Jadeed contempt case: Legal entity acquitted of all counts (STL, September 2015, upheld on appeal March 2016)
- Ayyash co-accused: Merhi, Oneissi, and Sabra all **acquitted** in STL August 2020 judgment — full acquittals with complete reasoning available in Stanford archive; demonstrates rigorous beyond-reasonable-doubt standard

### Procedural Rules Prosecutors Commonly Violate
From ICJ, HRW, Amnesty, and Legal Agenda documentation:
1. **Illegal detention beyond legal limits:** Court of Cassation criminal chamber has overturned convictions based on illegal pretrial detention
2. **Failure to present accused to investigative judge within 24-48 hours:** Lebanese Criminal Procedure Code Art. 47; prosecution routinely violates
3. **Denial of access to counsel during interrogation:** STL Rules establish stricter standard than Lebanese practice
4. **Military court jurisdiction over civilians:** No jurisdiction for non-military offenses per international standards; challenge via constitutional challenge and State Council jurisdiction objection
5. **Judicial Council referral by Cabinet:** No procedural rules or transparency standards; subject to challenge as arbitrary exercise of executive power
6. **Torture confessions:** Ziad Itani case and numerous OHCHR reports confirm confession obtained under torture is inadmissible; Shura Council reparations decision confirms state accountability

### Judges Tending to Rule Favorably on Defense Arguments
*Note: This is derived from publicly reported decisions; use cautiously*
- **Judge Claude Ghanem** (Government Commissioner to Military Court): Immediately appealed the Hezbollah weapons symbolic fine (March 2026) — shows willingness to enforce law impartially regardless of political pressure; **favorable signal for fair prosecution**
- **Judges in Beirut Indictment Chamber** handling Riad Salame case (Indictment Chamber President Kamal Nassar): Willingness to indict powerful figures suggests independence — may apply equal standards favorably to ordinary defendants
- **State Shura Council judges** in Ziad Itani case: Demonstrated willingness to rule against security establishment and apply anti-torture law — suggests administrative court is more independent than ordinary courts in security cases
- Judicial assignment decree published by Mahkama.net (April 2025) reveals current composition of all Court of Cassation chambers and all Courts of Appeal chambers — current as of 2025 judicial year

### Constitutional/Human Rights Grounds for Challenge
1. **Article 8, Lebanese Constitution:** Individual liberty guaranteed; no arrest except per law
2. **Lebanese Constitution Preamble (1990):** Universal Declaration of Human Rights is binding constitutional principle — opens door to UDHR-based arguments including fair trial rights
3. **ICCPR (ratified Lebanon 1972):** Arts. 9, 14, 15, 17 — due process, fair trial, legality, privacy
4. **CAT (ratified Lebanon 2000):** Convention Against Torture — Ziad Itani case shows Shura Council enforces this
5. **Anti-Torture Law No. 65/2017:** Domestic legislation criminalizing torture — confessions obtained under torture are inadmissible
6. **STL-derived constitutional arguments:** STL interlocutory jurisprudence on constitutionality of terrorism prosecution framework, right to fair trial, and standards of evidence
7. **Constitutional Council precedents:** Decisions annulling laws for violating right to social equality in taxation, retroactivity prohibition, procedure requirements — these constitutional standards extend to criminal statute challenges

## GAPS AND LIMITATIONS

1. **No centralized government database:** Lebanon has never created a comprehensive free online database of court decisions. This is a significant rule-of-law gap.
2. **Military Tribunal opacity:** No decisions are publicly available; all information comes from NGO monitoring and news reporting.
3. **Judicial Council secrecy:** As a final, no-appeal court for the most sensitive cases, the Judicial Council operates with zero transparency.
4. **Religious courts:** 18 separate confessional court systems with no online publication of decisions; only accessible through Eastlaws (subscription, incomplete) or physical archives.
5. **Subscription cost barriers:** The most comprehensive databases (Sader, IDREL) require paid subscriptions that may be expensive, particularly given Lebanon's economic collapse and currency depreciation.
6. **STL closure:** While the archive is preserved at Stanford, the website stl-tsl.org is now closed (confirmed: was shut down December 31, 2023); all links to the original STL website are dead and must be redirected to Stanford Virtual Tribunals.
7. **Bar disciplinary decisions:** Entirely opaque; not published; known only through media reports of specific cases.
8. **Arbitration awards:** Structurally confidential; no public access to any domestic LAMC awards.
9. **State Council portal**: The official portal (statecouncil.gov.lb/TreeView.aspx) has a search function but appears to contain little or no actual decisions in the online interface; decisions must be obtained through commercial databases or direct registry access.
10. **Lebanese economic crisis impact:** Several digital resources that may have been more functional pre-2019 may have degraded; institutional subscriptions at law schools and bar associations may have lapsed.
