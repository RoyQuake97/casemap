## 1. FRENCH MANDATE-ERA LEGISLATION (1920–1943)

### 1.1 Odyssée – Aix-Marseille Université: Recueil des actes administratifs du Haut-commissariat de la République française en Syrie et au Liban

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **URL** | https://odyssee.univ-amu.fr/items/show/4 |
| **Archive Name** | Bibliothèque numérique patrimoniale, Aix-Marseille Université (Odyssée) |
| **Historical Period Covered** | 1919–1926 (Recueil); Bulletin officiel: 1924–1941 |
| **Language(s)** | French |
| **Content Type & Depth** | 7 volumes + table (3,433 pages). Contains all general and imperative legislative acts (arrêtés/lois) issued by the French High Commissioner for Syria and Lebanon: administrative organization, judicial system, civil law, education, finance, customs, municipal organization. Excludes individual/private decisions. Provides comprehensive juridical, sociological, and economic overview of the territories under French Mandate. Source held at Archives nationales d'outre-mer (ANOM), Aix-en-Provence, cote 50507. |
| **Access Model** | **FREE – Public Domain.** Freely accessible online via Odyssée platform. PDF download available. |
| **Data Format** | PDF (7 volumes, 3,433 pages, in-4 format) |
| **Relevance to Current Lebanese Law Practice** | **CRITICAL.** Most Lebanese legislation in force today originated as High Commissioner arrêtés between 1919–1943. Code of Obligations and Contracts (1932), Code of Civil Procedure, real estate law (Decree 3339/1930), personal status framework (Decree 60LR/1936), commercial legislation – all traceable to this collection. Essential for any historical legal analysis. |
| **Unique/Rare Content** | The only digitized collection of the Recueil (as opposed to the Bulletin officiel). The Recueil was the curated compilation of laws with permanent legal force; the Bulletin included all administrative acts. This Odyssée version digitized from original ANOM holdings by Aix-Marseille University. Not duplicated elsewhere online. |

## CRITICAL GAPS AND NOTES FOR PRACTITIONERS

### What Does NOT Exist Online (Confirmed Gaps)

1. **Pre-1920 Official Gazette (Ottoman-era Lebanese publications):** The IDS/Lebanese University database claims coverage to 1915, but this is not publicly accessible online. No digitized Ottoman-era Official Gazette issues from Lebanese territory are confirmed available free online.

2. **Pre-1943 Lebanese Court Decisions:** No freely accessible online archive of Lebanese court decisions prior to independence. The Al-Mustashār and Sader databases contain these but only on a subscription/campus basis. The legallaw.ul.edu.lb portal has intermittent access and is not reliably online.

3. **Complete Digitized Run of the Lebanese Official Gazette (1943–1990):** No institution has put the complete 1943–1990 Official Gazette online in a freely accessible format. The Sader "free OGs platform" is the closest but access specifics and completeness are unclear.

4. **Civil War Period (1975–1990) Legal Documents:** Court decisions from conflict-zone courts, militia-issued "legislation," and territorial judicial decisions from this period are almost entirely unavailable digitally. Physical archives at Lebanese courts are poorly organized.

5. **French-Language Colonial Legal Doctrine:** The body of French legal scholarship specifically on Lebanese Mandate law (doctrine written by French jurists at CADN or USJ during 1920–1943) is not systematically digitized. Scattered items exist through Gallica and CADN but no comprehensive online collection.

### Key Observations on Access

- **Ottoman primary sources** (Land Code, Mecelle, Associations Law) are better digitized in **English translation** than in Arabic originals online.
- **Mandate-era sources** are primarily accessible in **French** through French institutional archives (Odyssée, MEAE Digital Library).
- **Arabic-language Mandate sources** are best through the ACO/NYU 3-volume compilation.
- **Historical court decisions** remain the most significant gap: commercial subscriptions (Sader, Al-Mustashār) dominate; no free comprehensive archive exists.
- **Post-Taif sources** (1990–present) are increasingly available through the Lebanese Official Gazette's digital version and through Sader.
