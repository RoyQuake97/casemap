## 9. Research Gaps & Notes

### Platforms Not Found / Confirmed Defunct
- **legislation.gov.lb** — Domain exists but returns no accessible legal content; no functional government legislation portal under this URL
- **IDREL / Al-Marjaa Cassandre** — Original web presence defunct; last references date to 2000s. Content may have been absorbed by Sader or other platforms
- **Qanoun.com** — No distinct Lebanese platform identified under this name; "qanoun" (قانون) is a generic Arabic word for law used by many platforms

### Access Barriers
- **Al-Mustashār fī al-Qānūn** — Critical resource accessible only at AUB Jafet Library Reference Desk; not remotely accessible even with institutional credentials
- **Official Gazette online** — Subscription model with unclear pricing post-currency collapse; the legiliban.ul.edu.lb database provides free access to the same content
- **Saderlex** — Subscription-based with no public pricing; the most comprehensive database is also the most commercially restricted

### Arabic-Language Search Findings (قاعدة بيانات قانونية لبنانية)
Arabic-language searches confirmed the same platforms identified above. No additional distinct Arabic-only Lebanese legal databases were uncovered beyond those cataloged. The Lebanese legal tech ecosystem remains dominated by Sader and the Lebanese University database.

### LexisNexis-Sader Partnership — Critical Note
LexisNexis Middle East explicitly identifies Sader Publishers as its Lebanon content partner and "the preferred translator of the Ministry of Justice in Lebanon." This makes Lexis Middle East's 87 Lebanese law documents authoritative English translations. However, Sader's own platform (Saderlex) contains dramatically more Lebanese content than what Lexis Middle East surfaces.

### Institutional Holdings Worth Noting
- **AUB Jafet Library** — Subscribes to Eastlaws, Saderlex, Al-Mustashār; has comprehensive Lebanese law collection
- **Library of Congress** — Maintains Foreign Law Web Archive including Lebanese Official Gazette and Legiliban; has physical collection of Lebanese law materials including al-Jarida al-Rasmiyya back to 1927
- **UNOG Library** — Maintains human rights-focused Lebanon guide

### Coverage Gaps in Existing Databases
1. **Lebanese court decisions** — No free, comprehensive public database of Lebanese court jurisprudence exists. Sader and Eastlaws hold the most extensive private collections. IDREL/Cassandre was the historic solution but is now defunct
2. **Personal status court decisions** — Sharia courts, ecclesiastical courts, and Druze courts issue decisions not systematically aggregated in any public database
3. **Shoura Council (State Council) administrative jurisprudence** — Not comprehensively covered in any free database
4. **Pre-1920 Ottoman-era Lebanese legal texts** — Limited coverage; best found in Al-Mustashār and Saderlex
5. **Lebanese arbitration awards** — The Lebanese Review of Arab and International Arbitration (in HeinOnline) covers some; no comprehensive free database

### Emerging Trends (2024–2026)
- **HAQQ Legal AI** (founded 2022, $3M raised February 2026) — Lebanon's first significant legal AI company. Building AI operating system for Arab legal markets with Lebanese law as foundational training data. 7,500+ clients across MENA, Europe, South America
- **Lebanese University's continuous updates** — The IP 77.42.251.205 database shows Laws 2/2025 and 3/2025 already indexed, suggesting active maintenance
- **ICRC presence update** — As of March 2, 2026, there is a new escalation of conflict in Lebanon; ICRC has been present since 1967 and is responding actively
