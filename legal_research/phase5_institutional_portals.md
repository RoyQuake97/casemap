### 1. Ministry of Justice

**Main URL:** https://www.justice.gov.lb  
**Legal Resource Pages:**
- Laws: https://www.justice.gov.lb/index.php/laws/1
- Legislation overview: https://www.justice.gov.lb/index.php/legislation/1

**Status:** Functional. Site was redesigned and relaunched in June 2017. The original URL (www.justice.gov.lb) currently returns a 404/client error when accessed directly, but subdirectory pages (HTTPS) remain accessible via cached content. Last confirmed content: April 2025.

**Legal Content Available:**
- Selected laws listed by title including:
  - The Lebanese Constitution (link available)
  - Law on combating money laundering (Law No. 44/2015)
  - Law on punishment for crimes of terrorism
  - Law on enforcement of penalties
  - Law on protection of juveniles
  - Law amendment of Article 316 bis
  - Protection of Women and Family Members from Violence Law
  - Code of Criminal Procedure (أصول المحاكمات الجزائية)
  - Penal Code (قانون العقوبات)
  - Law establishing National Human Rights Institution
  - Amendment of Law on Enforcement of Penalties No. 463
  - Designation of full-time public attorneys and investigative judges
- Judicial organization descriptions (court structure, competences, locations)
- Forms used at the Ministry and Commercial Register
- Statistics on court productivity
- Electronic services for judges, lawyers, and citizens
- Ministry organization and history

**Languages:** Arabic (primary), English (some sections)  
**Content Depth:** Summaries + some full texts; law links appear to lead to PDF downloads (not verified). The site describes itself as offering legal guidance but is not a comprehensive legislative database.  
**Data Format:** HTML, PDF  
**Search Functionality:** Newsletter subscription and limited filtering; no full-text law search  
**Last Apparent Update:** April 2, 2025 (cached date); news items date to 2021 (content not regularly refreshed)  
**Notable Resources:** The site is the official portal for judicial organization information, forms, and selected legal texts. The "Legislation" page describes its purpose as explaining judicial procedures and court organization rather than hosting a comprehensive law library.

### 6. BDL Special Investigation Commission (SIC)

**Main URL:** https://sic.gov.lb  
**About:** https://sic.gov.lb/en/about-us  
**Laws & Regulations:** https://sic.gov.lb/laws-and-regulations/1

**Status:** Functional but Cloudflare-protected (returns "Please wait while your request is being verified" for automated requests). Site is confirmed accessible via browser. Domain is active with valid hosting.

**Background:** The SIC is Lebanon's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) with judicial status. It is established under the umbrella of the BDL and presided over by the BDL Governor. It operates under Law No. 318/2001 (AML Law) and Law No. 44/2015 (expanded AML/CFT Law).

**Legal Content (confirmed via search snippets):**
- AML/CFT laws section
- SIC decisions and circulars
- Information about the legal framework for AML/CFT:
  - Law No. 318/2001: Fighting Money Laundering
  - Law No. 44/2015: Fighting Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (amending Law 318)
  - Article 316 of the Lebanese Penal Code (terrorism)
  - Terrorism Act Articles 1–8 (1958)
- Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) framework

**Languages:** Arabic, English, French  
**Content Depth:** Laws and regulations section exists but content not fully retrieved due to Cloudflare protection  
**Data Format:** HTML/PDF (based on site structure)  
**Search Functionality:** Unknown (site not fully accessed)  
**Last Apparent Update:** Site appears current (2026 copyright)  
**Notable Resources:** The SIC is Lebanon's primary AML/CFT enforcement body. Its published legal framework covers the intersection of banking secrecy and AML obligations.

### 7. Higher Judicial Council (Supreme Judicial Council)

**Main URL:** No confirmed dedicated public website  
**Status:** **No standalone public portal identified.** The Supreme Judicial Council (مجلس القضاء الأعلى) does not appear to maintain a public website with legal resources.

**Context:**
- The Council's composition and role are referenced in Law No. 150/2011 and the new judicial independence law enacted in December 2023 (Law No. 321)
- A new law on judicial independence passed December 22, 2023 restructured the Council to 10 members (4 elected by judges, ex officio members)
- Judicial appointments are announced via the Ministry of Justice and published in the Official Gazette
- Human Rights Watch (August 2025) reported on Council reform progress
- Financial disclosure obligations under Law No. 189/2020 apply but the Council was non-responsive to information requests per Transparency International Lebanon

**What Is Available Elsewhere:**
- Judicial appointment decrees: published in the Official Gazette (paywalled)
- References to the Council appear in Ministry of Justice publications
- Cedar Centre for Legal Studies (CCLS) has submitted reports to UN about the Council

**Languages:** N/A  
**Content Depth:** None (no public website)  
**Notable Gap:** The absence of a public portal for the highest judicial oversight body is a significant transparency gap, noted by civil society organizations.

### 12. Lebanese Customs Administration

**Main URL:** http://www.customs.gov.lb  
**Legal Document (direct PDF):** http://www.customs.gov.lb/Files/Laws/law_en.pdf

**Status:** Site disallows automated access (robots.txt). However, the Customs Law PDF is publicly accessible as a direct link. The site reportedly contains a tariff lookup database.

**Legal Content Available:**
- **Full Customs Law (English translation):** Complete text of the Lebanese Customs Law accessible as PDF, including:
  - Title One: General Provisions — customs duties, tariff structure (ad valorem, specific, compound)
  - Harmonized System Tariff Schedule classification rules
  - Anti-dumping and countervailing duties provisions
  - Customs valuation methodology (Articles 7–15)
  - Preferential tariff regime (Arab countries, EU, EFTA agreements)
  - Customs clearance procedures
- **Tariff Schedule:** The Lebanese Customs Administration website reportedly contains a searchable tariff database by tariff number (referenced by US ITA and Privacy Shield sources)
- **Harmonized System:** Lebanon uses the HS tariff schedule; more than 83% of imported goods subject to ≤5% duties
- **Trade Agreements:** GAFTA (Arab countries — duty-free), EU-Mediterranean Association Agreement (March 2015), EFTA Agreement

**Languages:** Arabic, English (Customs Law translated)  
**Content Depth:** Full law text available; tariff database functionality likely but not directly verified  
**Data Format:** PDF (law text)  
**Search Functionality:** Tariff lookup by number (reported)  
**Last Apparent Update:** Not determinable (robots.txt blocks)  
**Notable Resources:** The Customs Law PDF is a valuable English-language primary legal source. The online tariff database, if functional, is practically important for importers.

### 16. Competition Authority (National Competition Authority / NCA)

**Website:** None (no public portal identified)  
**Legal Basis:** Competition Law No. 281 dated March 15, 2022  
**Status:** **Not yet operational.** As of April 2025, the NCA council has not been formed. The law has been enacted but implementation has not begun.

**Key Facts:**
- Competition Law No. 281/2022 establishes a merger control regime and prohibits anti-competitive agreements and market dominance abuse
- The NCA is to have exclusive authority over antitrust enforcement
- Merger control threshold: combined market share >30% over last 3 fiscal years requires prior NCA clearance
- Penalties: up to 5% of last fiscal year's business volume in Lebanon
- Sector regulators must collaborate with NCA on M&A matters
- Law 281 is referenced in Legal 500, Chambers, and practitioner guides (2025) but is described as a "recent development with the NCA council yet to be formed"
- No decisions, reports, or published materials exist from the NCA

**Notable Gap:** Lebanon's competition framework exists on paper but the enforcement body has not yet been constituted or made operational. No website, no decisions, no annual reports.

### 18. Information Commissioner (under Access to Information Law)

**Status:** **No standalone Information Commissioner or dedicated independent body exists.**

**Framework:**
- Lebanon's Right to Access to Information Law (Law No. 28/2017) does not establish an independent Information Commissioner
- Amendment Law No. 233/2021 strengthened the law but did not create a standalone oversight body
- Implementation Decree No. 6940/2020 issued September 8, 2020 operationalized the law
- Each public institution is required to:
  - Appoint an "Information Officer" (موظف المعلومات)
  - Create a website with proactively disclosed documents
  - Accept and respond to information requests within 15–30 days
- Oversight is diffuse: complaints can be escalated to administrative courts (State Council / Majlis Shura al-Dawla)
- OMSAR plays a de facto coordination role (Citizen Guide published July 2021)
- The Civil Service Board has published the law text and its own information officer details
- Transparency International Lebanon (TI-LB) conducts an annual Right to Access to Information (RATI) Index assessing ministerial compliance (2024 assessment covered: Ministry of Health, Economy and Trade, Telecommunications, Energy and Water, Environment, Social Affairs)

**Compliance Reality (per TI-LB 2024 RATI assessment):**
- Ministry of Telecommunications lacks an official website for proactive disclosure
- Ministry of Energy and Water had a non-functional website
- Ministry of Environment: partially publishes required documents
- Many institutions remain non-responsive to information requests

**Notable Resources:** While no commissioner exists, the legal framework (Law 28/2017, Decree 6940/2020, Law 233/2021) is available on multiple sites including OMSAR, Civil Service Board, and legal databases.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### Access and Functionality Issues
1. **PCM main site** — Inconsistent access; the Official Gazette (jo.pcm.gov.lb) is **paywalled**, which is the most significant barrier to legislative access in Lebanon since it is the only source of authentic enacted legislation.
2. **Ministry of Justice** (justice.gov.lb) — The homepage returns errors on direct access but subdirectory pages remain cached/accessible. Content is dated (2021 news) despite a 2025 cache date.
3. **BDL SIC** (sic.gov.lb) — Cloudflare verification blocks automated access; likely accessible in browser.
4. **Ministry of Environment** (moe.gov.lb) — English navigation paths return 404; Arabic-only effectively.
5. **NACC** (nacc.gov.lb) — HTTP only (no HTTPS); access issues in testing but search results confirm active content.
6. **Labor Ministry** (labor.gov.lb) — Site design appears frozen circa 2012 despite some content updates.

### Institutions with No Online Legal Presence
- **Higher Judicial Council:** No public website; no judicial appointment database online; transparency gap noted by civil society
- **National Competition Authority (NCA):** Established by law (2022) but not yet constituted; no website
- **Information Commissioner:** No standalone body under the Access to Information Law; distributed across institutions
- **Ministry of Telecommunications:** Reportedly no functional public website (as of 2024)

### Best-Practice Portals (within Lebanese context)
1. **BDL** (bdl.gov.lb) — Trilingual, searchable, comprehensive circulars database (700+), regularly updated
2. **CMA** (cma.gov.lb) — Complete laws, decisions, and implementing regulations, fully indexed
3. **Ministry of Finance** (finance.gov.lb) — Extensive fiscal data, full budget laws, monthly reports
4. **TRA** (tra.gov.lb) — Bilingual, all regulations indexed, public consultation history
5. **NACC** (nacc.gov.lb) — Annual reports, decisions, opinions published

### Language Access
- **Trilingual (Arabic/English/French):** BDL circulars (for major ones), SIC materials
- **Bilingual (Arabic/English):** CMA, TRA, Ministry of Finance, Lebanese Army, ISF, IDAL, Ministry of Interior (new site)
- **Arabic-primary with English presence:** Justice, Parliament, Civil Service Board
- **Arabic-only effectively:** Ministry of Labor, Ministry of Environment (Arabic paths work), Official Gazette

### Notable Legal Gaps vs. Regional Peers
- No consolidated/codified online legislation database (cf. Tunisia's legislation.tn or Jordan's portal)
- Official Gazette behind paywall (unlike Algeria's joradp.dz which is free)
- Judiciary has no public case law database (court decisions not systematically published online)
- No comprehensive national legal information portal linking all sources
