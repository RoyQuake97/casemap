- Accessing all treaty body concluding observations criticizing Lebanese law and practice
- Identifying gaps in Lebanon's human rights obligations that can be argued before domestic courts
- Obtaining shadow reports from Lebanese and international NGOs documenting specific violations
- Challenging military court jurisdiction, detention practices, torture, and death penalty under ratified treaties

### 1.4 Regional Arab Treaties

#### Arab League Treaty Collection

**URL:** https://treaties.un.org/pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=080000028015a44b  
**Organization:** League of Arab States / UN Treaty Collection  
**Content:** Pact of the League of Arab States (1945); Lebanon ratified 16/05/1945, in force 01/06/1945  
**Joint Defence and Economic Co-Operation Treaty** — Lebanon is a signatory (1950)

#### Arab Charter on Human Rights (2004)

**URL:** https://www.refworld.org/legal/agreements/las/1994/en/10672  
**Full text (University of Minnesota):** https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/instree/arabhrcharter.html  
**Organization:** League of Arab States  
**Status:** Lebanon has **NOT ratified** the 2004 Arab Charter on Human Rights (only Algeria, Bahrain, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Libya, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Syria, UAE, Yemen had ratified as of 2013). Lebanon is not among the ratifying states.  
**Note:** The 1994 version of the Arab Charter was never widely ratified; the 2004 revised version entered into force 2008.  
**Relevance:** The Arab Charter includes protections relevant to Lebanese defense practice (Articles on fair trial, torture, detention), and its monitoring body (Arab Human Rights Committee) produces country reports. Lebanon's non-ratification limits applicability but the Charter can still be cited as a regional standard.

#### Arab Charter on Human Rights Monitoring Body

**URL:** https://www.ohchr.org/en/treaty-bodies/arab-charter-human-rights  
**Type:** Periodic reporting and review mechanism for state parties  
**Relevance:** Lebanon's non-ratification noted; can be cited as a benchmark for regional standards

A comprehensive alphabetical catalog of ~208 treaties ratified by Lebanon covering: ILO conventions, humanitarian law, environmental conventions, maritime law, aviation, intellectual property, commerce, nuclear safety, and more.

#### Content of Venice Commission Opinions on Lebanon

**2022 Opinion (Draft Law on Independence of Judicial Courts):**
- Requested by Minister of Justice Marie Claude Najm (September 2021)
- Reviews proposed replacement of Decree-Law No. 150/1983 on the organization of the judiciary
- Key findings: Art. 20 Constitution guarantees judicial independence but is silent on appointment method; confessional principle creates tension with merit-based appointments; Higher Judicial Council (SCM) composition problematic; executive branch retains excessive influence; lack of security of tenure
- Recommends: Majority of SCM members should be elected by judicial peers; detailed and objective criteria for all appointments; HJC should control its own budget

**2024 Opinion (Draft Law on Administrative Judiciary):**
- Requested by Minister of Justice Henri Khoury (June 2023)
- Reviews draft Law on Administrative Judiciary to establish a separate administrative court system
- Key findings: Confessionalism not addressed in draft Law; competitive procedure for selection (Article 34) is positive; ECtHR jurisprudence cited as relevant international standard even though Lebanon is not an ECHR member; composition of High Council of Administrative Justice (HCAJ) raises concerns
- Recommends: Increase judges elected by peers; better representation of lower-court judges; consider adding lay members

**Relevance to Defense Practice:**
- Both opinions are directly persuasive on questions of judicial independence, prosecutorial independence, and the legitimacy of the Higher Judicial Council
- Cite ICCPR Art. 14, Arab Charter Art. 12, and ECtHR standards as applicable to Lebanon
- Highly useful for arguing that current judicial appointment and discipline mechanisms violate international standards

## 5. SPECIAL TRIBUNAL FOR LEBANON (STL)

**Primary Website (archived):** https://www.stl-tsl.org [NOTE: STL closed December 31, 2023; website captured by Stanford Libraries on December 7, 2023]  
**UN Archives:** Archives transferred to UN Archives and Records Management Section (ARMS) upon closure  
**Stanford Virtual Tribunals Collection:** https://humanrights.stanford.edu/news/virtual-tribunals-debuts-special-tribunal-lebanon-collection  
**Organization:** Special Tribunal for Lebanon (international tribunal, Leidschendam, Netherlands)  
**Operational Period:** March 1, 2009 – December 31, 2023  
**Languages:** Arabic, English, French (all three official working languages)  
**Access Model:** Free (all public documents)  
**Data Format:** PDF, HTML, multimedia

### Background and Jurisdiction

Established pursuant to UN Security Council Resolution 1757 (2007), acting under Chapter VII. Applied Lebanese criminal law (Lebanese Criminal Code, particularly Articles 314-316 on terrorism, Articles 547-550 on intentional homicide) with internationally-inspired procedural rules. First international tribunal to:
- Exercise jurisdiction over terrorism as a distinct crime
- Allow trials in absentia
- Convict a corporate entity (media company)

**STL Statute:** https://www.stl-tsl.org/en/documents/stl-statute (now archived)  
**STL Rules of Procedure and Evidence (February 2015):** Available in Arabic, English, French  
**GlobaLex Research Guide on STL:** https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/special_tribunal_lebanon1.html

### Cases at the STL

#### Main Case: Prosecutor v. Ayyash et al. (STL-11-01)

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Trial began | January 16, 2014 (in absentia) |
| Verdict | August 18, 2020 |
| Outcome | Salim Ayyash convicted (5 counts: conspiracy, terrorist act, intentional homicide of Hariri and 21 others); 3 defendants (Badreddine, Oneissi, Sabra) acquitted |
| Appeal Judgment (Merhi/Oneissi) | March 2022 — Summary: https://www.legal-tools.org/doc/his62c |

#### Connected Cases (STL-11-02): Hamadeh, Hawi and El-Murr

#### Contempt Cases (Corporate Liability)

|------|-----------|---------|

#### Other Proceedings

- **El Sayed matter:** Request by Gen. Jamil El Sayed for release of investigative documents; complex litigation 2010-2015
- **El Hajj matter:** Similar request for evidentiary materials (2015)

### Key Legal Precedents from STL Relevant to Lebanese Defense Practice

1. **Definition of terrorism under Lebanese law** — Interlocutory Decision of the Appeals Chamber, February 16, 2011 (on 15 legal questions including definition of terrorism, conspiracy, homicide, perpetration, cumulative charging): landmark interpretation of Lebanese Penal Code Articles 314-316 applicable to all Lebanese terrorism prosecutions
2. **Trial in absentia** — STL developed extensive jurisprudence on due process rights of accused tried in absentia
3. **Corporate criminal liability** — First international tribunal to hold a corporate entity criminally liable
4. **Telecommunications evidence** — Over 3,000 exhibits; 300 witnesses; 85,000+ pages of transcript; legal framework for telecom data as criminal evidence

### STL Archive and Post-Closure Access

**Stanford Virtual Tribunals Initiative** (July 17, 2024 launch):  
- Released 64 most essential STL files (most vital to preserving legal precedent)
- Full 15,000-document collection to follow
- Fully text-searchable; metadata-encoded; available in Arabic, English, French
- Archived STL website captured December 7, 2023
- URL: https://humanrights.stanford.edu/news/virtual-tribunals-debuts-special-tribunal-lebanon-collection

**UN Archives and Records Management Section (ARMS):**  
All STL archives transferred to UN ARMS upon December 31, 2023 closure.

**Refworld STL Source Page:** https://www.refworld.org/document-sources/special-tribunal-lebanon  
Provides access to STL decisions via UNHCR's legal database.

### Relevance to Lebanese Defense Practice

- All terrorism charges in Lebanon are based on the same Lebanese Criminal Code articles the STL interpreted
- STL's interlocutory decision on terrorism definition is binding interpretive authority for Lebanese courts hearing terrorism cases
- Fair trial standards developed at STL exceed what Lebanese military and civil courts typically apply
- STL jurisprudence on victim participation, rights of the accused, evidence, and in absentia trials provides comparative benchmarks

### Key Arguments Enabled by These Sources

1. **Military court jurisdiction over civilians** — Violates CCPR Art. 14 (confirmed in 1997 and 2018 Concluding Observations); echoed in CAT and UPR; Venice Commission opinion supports reform
2. **Torture and detention conditions** — CAT treaty body observations; OPCAT ratification (2009) requires independent monitoring; shadow reports from ALEF, CLDH, Human Rights Watch provide factual support
3. **Death penalty** — Lebanon retains death penalty (84 on death row, 2025); CCPR observations urge abolition; CCPR-OP2 not ratified but ICCPR Art. 6 still applies; Special Rapporteur observations from 2025
4. **Judicial independence** — Venice Commission directly addresses inadequacy of current framework; CCPR/HRC has noted executive interference since 1997; FATF grey-listing cites judicial independence deficiencies
5. **Criminalization of consensual same-sex relations** (Art. 534 Penal Code) — CCPR CO/3 (2018) specifically calls for decriminalization; CAT observations note police targeting
6. **Gender discrimination / personal status laws** — CEDAW CO/6 (2022) comprehensive critique; 16-sect personal status system incompatible with CEDAW and ICCPR
7. **Palestinian refugee rights** — CERD observations; CCPR observations; no Refugee Convention ratification but customary non-refoulement applies
8. **Access to justice / legal aid** — World Bank assessment (2003/2004) documents structural barriers; CCPR/CAT observations; confirmed by FATF grey-listing
9. **Kafala (migrant domestic worker sponsorship system)** — CEDAW, CERD, CCPR observations all identify kafala as incompatible with international standards; ILO Forced Labour Convention ratified (C. 029)
10. **Right to fair trial** — STL jurisprudence establishes higher standards applicable to terrorism cases; CCPR Art. 14 violations documented across multiple concluding observations cycles
